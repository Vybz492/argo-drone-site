Breaking the endurance barrier in autonomous aviation.

Our mission

The power to go further.
On hydrogen.

Our vision is to change the future of autonomous aviation through the development of sustainable, zero-emission and long-endurance flight systems.

The hydrogen advantage

01

Maximum energy density

Hydrogen holds significantly more energy per kilogram than any other chemical fuel (approx. 33 kWh/kg compared to 0.25 kWh/kg for lithium batteries). Because the fuel is fundamentally lighter, powertrain weight is drastically reduced, meaning less power is wasted lifting dead weight and hours of continuous flight are unlocked.

02

True zero-emission

Electricity is generated through a clean chemical reaction within the fuel cell. The only byproduct exhausted during flight is pure water vapor, allowing for completely emission-free operations.

03

Continuous efficiency

Instead of constantly landing an aircraft to swap and recharge multiple battery packs across a single mission, one hydrogen fuel tank provides sufficient energy to cover vast areas in a single, uninterrupted flight. When refueling is finally required, it is completed in minutes.

04

Meeting industrial demand

As global industries increasingly require continuous, extended flight times for automated data collection, hydrogen drivetrains deliver the sustained endurance that conventional technologies cannot fulfill.

Simulation

The critical mass penalty.

We modelled in MATLAB how heavy a drone's energy system must be for a given flight time. Batteries add about 4 kg per hour. Hydrogen starts at 2 kg but adds only 0.4 kg per hour, so past 33 minutes it is the lighter option.

At our 7⁠–⁠8 hour target, batteries would weigh 28⁠–⁠32 kg, more than the 25 kg the whole drone may weigh. Hydrogen would weigh about 5 kg.

The story

Proving the powertrain on a global stage.

To validate the hydrogen architecture, a problem had to be solved that conventional drones physically cannot fix.

As global industries strive to achieve net-zero targets, significant investments are made into environmental offsets to neutralize corporate carbon footprints. Consequently, billions of dollars are traded in the carbon offset market based on rough satellite estimates. These estimates only capture the top of the canopy and fail to measure true biomass density underneath. To accurately map 3D structures and calculate exact carbon storage, advanced LiDAR systems must be flown low and slow over vast areas. Operating these complex sensors requires a high continuous power draw. Standard battery drones deplete their energy rapidly under these conditions, forcing landings within 15 minutes, while helicopters remain too expensive and highly polluting.

By utilizing a hydrogen hexacopter, high-precision carbon auditing is made economically viable. The massive energy capacity allows for hours of continuous flight, establishing a new standard for verified environmental data. This capability is applied in two core areas:

High-precision carbon auditing

01

Terrestrial auditing

Dual-laser LiDAR is carried low above forestry projects to penetrate foliage gaps. This maps the complete 3D structure of the forest floor, calculating the exact wood volume and stored carbon.

02

Blue carbon auditing

Coastal ecosystems, such as kelp forests and mangroves, are mapped using bathymetric lasers that penetrate the water column. The extended endurance of the hydrogen system ensures that these advanced, high-draw sensors are safely flown over water for hours without the constant risk of battery depletion.

Current project: the hexacopter testbed

Developing a hybrid powertrain architecture to bridge the gap between theoretical and physical reality.

To balance sustained energy capacity with dynamic power delivery, the powertrain is designed as a parallel hybrid system. The architecture is driven by the following integrated components:

Planned architecture

01

Hydrogen storage

Hydrogen is compressed and stored within a Type IV Carbon tank, providing the primary, lightweight energy reserve for the system.

02

PEM fuel cell

The stored hydrogen is supplied to a Proton Exchange Membrane (PEM) fuel cell, generating a steady, continuous supply of DC power intended for sustained flight.

03

Power management

A DC/DC converter functions as the central controller, safely limiting and regulating the current distributed across the internal DC bus.

04

Buffer battery

A smaller battery is integrated in parallel to absorb sudden power spikes. While the fuel cell delivers steady baseline energy, the buffer battery instantly handles the volatile power demands required by the motors and electronic speed controllers (ESCs) during dynamic maneuvers.

Why parallel

Parallel, not series.

In a series setup the battery feeds the motors on its own, so take-off and every gust push it past its safe limit.

In our parallel setup the fuel cell supplies a steady 650 W alongside it. The battery only covers the peaks, stays under its limit for the whole two hours and recharges during cruise.

Future project: the fixed-wing VTOL

Combining hydrogen endurance with aerodynamic efficiency for apex industrial operations.

Once the hybrid powertrain is fully validated, the drivetrain will be integrated into a custom-designed fixed-wing VTOL (Vertical Take-Off and Landing) airframe. This aerodynamic configuration minimizes drag and maximizes forward flight efficiency, transforming the power system into a long-range platform capable of flying continuously for hundreds of kilometers.

This extended range unlocks autonomous, non-stop linear monitoring, directly replacing highly expensive legacy aviation solutions across prestigious industries:

  • 01

    Thermal inspections for high-voltage power grids

  • 02

    Laser scanning for high-speed railways

  • 03

    Autonomous safety patrols for gas pipelines

  • 04

    Automated calibration for airport landing systems

  • 05

    High-speed surface mapping for motorways
