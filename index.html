Breaking the endurance barrier in autonomous aviation.

Discovery Team · TU/e innovation Space · Est. 2026

Our mission

The power to go further.
On hydrogen.

Our vision is to change the future of autonomous aviation through the development of sustainable, zero-emission and long-endurance flight systems.

The Hydrogen Advantage

33 kWh/kg

Maximum energy density

Hydrogen stores more energy per kilogram than any other chemical fuel, so less power is spent lifting the energy store and hours of continuous flight become possible.

H₂O

True zero-emission

Electricity is generated by a chemical reaction within the fuel cell, and the only byproduct emitted during flight is water vapour.

Minutes to refuel

Continuous efficiency

A single hydrogen tank provides enough energy to cover large areas in one uninterrupted flight, without repeated landings to exchange battery packs, and refuelling is completed in minutes.

Hours of flight

Meeting industrial demand

Industries increasingly require continuous, extended flight times for automated data collection, and hydrogen drivetrains are able to deliver the sustained endurance that battery drones cannot.

Simulation

The critical mass penalty.

A MATLAB model was developed to determine how heavy a drone's energy system must be for a given flight time. A battery system adds approximately 4 kg for every hour of flight, whereas a hydrogen system starts at 2 kg and adds only approximately 0.4 kg per hour. Beyond approximately 33 minutes, hydrogen is therefore the lighter option.

At our target of 7⁠–⁠8 hours of flight, the batteries alone would weigh 28⁠–⁠32 kg, exceeding the 25 kg limit for the entire drone. The hydrogen system would weigh approximately 5 kg.

The story

High-precision carbon auditing, on land and at sea.

To validate the hydrogen architecture, we have chosen a problem that conventional drones physically cannot solve: measuring the biomass that carbon credits are supposed to represent.

The unsolved problemThe carbon market trades on biomass that nobody can measure at scale.

As companies work towards net-zero emissions targets, billions of dollars in carbon credits are traded on the assumption that a specific forest or marine ecosystem absorbs a specific amount of CO₂. Yet the true biomass, the actual volume of trunks, branches and underwater flora, cannot yet be measured accurately at scale.

Measuring it requires a heavy, high-grade LiDAR system flown extremely low and slow over the canopy or the water surface, for hours, to map its 3D structure. No current aircraft can do this economically, so the market relies on rough satellite estimates.

  • Inaccurate data
  • Significant financial risk
  • Corporate greenwashing

Why current methods fall short

  • Satellite

    Sees only the top

    Satellites see only the top of the canopy or the surface of the water, not the density of what lies beneath.

  • Helicopter

    Too expensive and polluting

    Flies low and slow and carries 3⁠–⁠7 kg for hours, but is too expensive and polluting for routine audits.

  • Battery drone

    Lands within about 15 minutes

    Flies low and slow but can barely carry 3⁠–⁠7 kg and lands within approximately 15 minutes. It cannot sustain the power for a heavy payload, so not even one hectare can be mapped.

  • Fixed-wing drone

    Flies too fast

    Carries 3⁠–⁠7 kg for hours, but flies too fast for dense LiDAR penetration.

The solutionThe hydrogen hexacopter.

A heavy-lift hydrogen hexacopter would carry heavy LiDAR low and slow for hours, making high-frequency, centimetre-accurate carbon auditing economically viable. Its fuel cell would provide the endurance and payload capacity this requires, establishing a new standard of verified data for the carbon offset market.

Target markets and operationsTwo markets, one airframe.

Terrestrial

Sub-canopy carbon auditing

Dual-laser LiDAR would map the full 3D structure of forests, from the canopy down to the forest floor.

More details
Target
Forestry projects, carbon registries and green investment funds.
Operation
The drone would fly low and slow above the tree canopy, carrying a heavy dual-laser LiDAR system.
Data output
The lasers pass through gaps in the foliage to map the forest floor and trunks in 3D, after which algorithms compute the exact wood volume and, from it, the carbon stored.

Marine and coastal

Blue carbon auditing

Green-laser LiDAR would map kelp forests, seagrass meadows and mangroves through the water column.

More details
Target
Kelp forests, seagrass meadows and mangrove conservation projects, which absorb CO₂ much faster than forests on land.
Operation
The drone would fly low over ocean, coastline or swamp, carrying a heavy bathymetric (green-laser) LiDAR system that penetrates the water column.
Data output
3D maps of seagrass and kelp beds and of mangrove root systems, while hours of endurance would keep expensive sensors safe over water, without the constant risk of battery depletion.
