01 / Why hydrogen

Battery drones land after 30 minutes. We want to stay up for 60.

To double a battery drone's flight time you almost double its battery, and the extra weight eats most of the gain. Hydrogen breaks that loop: more flight time costs a slightly bigger tank and a few hundred grams of gas.

That's the raw fuel. Once you add the tank, fuel cell and cooling, the real gap shrinks a lot. Closing it is the engineering problem we're working on.

The endurance intercept

Short flights favour batteries. Past roughly 45 minutes, the fixed weight of a fuel cell pays for itself, and our one-hour target sits in hydrogen territory. Illustrative curve, not measured data.

Where it matters

Inspection

Infrastructure

Pipelines, power lines and offshore wind farms in a single sortie instead of ten battery swaps.

Large-scale farming

Long imaging runs over big fields, with a refuel in minutes instead of a recharge in hours.

02 / Our approach

Not just a drone. A power plant that flies.

Anyone can buy a fuel cell. Making it survive a real flight is what's hard. Our focus is the drivetrain: the hardware and control logic between the hydrogen tank and the motors.

01

Fuel cell runs steady

Fuel cells degrade under sudden load changes. Ours holds a steady, efficient output, which protects the membrane and the hydrogen budget.

02

Battery takes the spikes

Take-off, gusts and manoeuvres cause sharp power peaks. A buffer battery covers them and recharges when demand drops.

03

Electronics in charge

An active DC/DC converter decides how much current the fuel cell delivers and reports to the flight controller in real time.

04

Airframe-agnostic

The drivetrain is a self-contained module. Swap the airframe, keep the power system. One design, many missions.

Five phases from paper to a one-hour hydrogen flight. We update this as we go.

    Research

    Fuel cells, hydrogen storage, safety and drivetrain architecture.

    Done

  2. Phase 02

    Testbed

    A battery-powered testbed drone to learn flight control, telemetry and tuning.

    In progress

  3. Phase 03

    Bench test

    Fuel cell and buffer battery on the bench. Hybrid control logic in MATLAB first, then in hardware.

    Next

  4. Phase 04

    Integration

    Drivetrain module in the airframe: thermal routing, packaging and safety systems.

    Planned

  5. Phase 05

    Sixty minutes in the air on hydrogen, under 25 kg.
