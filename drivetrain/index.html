The Kingfisher Project

Bridging the gap between theoretical and physical reality.

Our testbed is a DJI S800 EVO hexacopter. Its powertrain would combine a hydrogen fuel cell, which supplies steady power, with a small battery that covers sudden peaks in demand. Each component in the diagram below can be selected and examined in detail.

Future project: the fixed-wing VTOL

Combining hydrogen endurance with aerodynamic efficiency.

Once the hybrid powertrain has been fully validated, the drivetrain will be integrated into a custom-designed fixed-wing VTOL (vertical take-off and landing) airframe. This aerodynamic configuration minimises drag and maximises forward-flight efficiency, turning the power system into a long-range platform capable of flying continuously for hundreds of kilometres.

This range would enable autonomous, non-stop monitoring of infrastructure, replacing expensive crewed helicopters and aircraft. The five use cases shown here are separate applications that share one problem, costly inspection work in major industries, and the VTOL is planned as a modular drone that would be configured differently for each of them.
